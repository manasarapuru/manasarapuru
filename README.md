### 🎯 My Drive

Modern biology moves faster than the tools built to support it. For decades, computational biology focused on generating, storing, and analyzing biological data at ever greater resolution. These advances allowed researchers to measure biology in ways that were previously impossible, from sequencing genomes and transcriptomes to profiling individual cells. The next challenge is no longer only generating and analyzing biological data, but turning those measurements into actionable insights that can guide experiments, identify therapeutic opportunities, and accelerate discovery.

That shift depends on bringing together two different kinds of intelligence. Researchers bring biological context, scientific judgment, and the ability to recognize when a result has meaning beyond the data itself. AI systems bring the ability to process massive amounts of information, identify patterns across complex datasets, and explore possibilities at a scale that humans cannot. Neither replaces the other. The real design question is not how to make biological tools simply faster or more powerful, but how to build systems where these strengths work together.

As AI systems become capable of contributing to scientific workflows, the next generation of bioinformatics tools will require more than models that demonstrate performance in a notebook. They will require systems that allow researchers to interact with, evaluate, and build upon AI capabilities as part of their everyday work.

The goal is to move beyond interfaces that simply allow researchers to run models and toward ecosystems where scientists can collaborate with AI. These systems should connect computational outputs with biological reasoning, enabling researchers to question results, apply context, and translate predictions into meaningful discoveries. The future of AI in biology will not be defined only by what models can generate, but by how effectively humans can work alongside them.

I aim to build tools where functionality, scalability, and usability evolve together. This means pairing biological foundation models with the machine learning and software engineering needed to transform powerful computational capabilities into systems that researchers can meaningfully incorporate into their workflows.

This interdisciplinary field that sits at the intersection of biology, AI, engineering, and human-centered design is still emerging. My interests lie in understanding what intuitive, trustworthy, and human-centered tool development looks like for the next generation of computational biology.

### 🎯 What to Expect Here

This GitHub is a collection of design explorations and technical prototypes built around real challenges in computational biology. Each project begins with a workflow I have encountered in research or biotechnology and asks how that workflow changes as AI becomes a collaborator rather than just another tool.

Some projects focus on redesigning scientific interfaces. Others explore biological foundation models, agentic workflows, or machine learning applications. Many combine all of these disciplines into a single prototype.

Rather than asking, *"Can AI perform this task?"*, these projects ask questions like:

* How should researchers interact with increasingly autonomous systems?
* What information should remain visible when AI performs part of a workflow?
* How can computational outputs become scientific reasoning instead of just predictions?
* What makes an AI enabled scientific tool trustworthy enough to support real research?

Every repository is an experiment toward answering one of those questions.

The projects below are organized around recurring challenges in computational biology rather than programming languages or technologies. Each explores a different aspect of how AI, software engineering, and human centered design can support scientific discovery.

### 🔍 Research Agenda

My work explores how AI, software engineering, and human-centered design can come together to support the next generation of computational biology. Rather than organizing projects around individual technologies, I focus on the broader question of how scientific workflows evolve when AI becomes an active collaborator in research.


#### 1. Scientific Workflows 🔬

- **How should biological workflows evolve as AI systems become capable of planning, executing, and interpreting parts of the research process?**

- **Questions I explore:**

  * **How can AI agents support multi-step computational biology workflows?**
    * Exploring how AI systems can move beyond isolated tasks toward helping researchers plan analyses, coordinate tools, and navigate complex scientific processes.

  * **What should remain under researcher control as systems become more autonomous?**
    * Exploring how to balance automation with human expertise, including where biological context, scientific judgment, and decision-making remain essential.

  * **How can workflows make AI actions transparent, reversible, and trustworthy?**
    * Exploring how researchers can understand, evaluate, and guide AI-generated actions before incorporating them into scientific decisions.

- **Examples (Projects):**

  * AI-assisted bioinformatics pipelines
     * ```#1 AI-assisted RNA-seq workflow```
     * ```#2 Automated analysis pipeline assistant```
  * Agentic analysis workflows
    * ```#1 Multi-step bioinformatics agent```
    * ```#2 Agent workflow planner ```
  * Research planning and decision support systems
    * ```#1 Experimental design assistant```



#### 2. Scientific Interfaces 🎨

- **How should researchers interact with increasingly complex computational systems?**

- **Questions I explore:**

  * **How can scientific tools preserve complexity without overwhelming researchers?**
    * Exploring how interfaces can reveal necessary information at the right time while allowing deeper exploration when needed.

  * **How should interfaces change when AI becomes part of the scientific workflow?**
    * Exploring how interaction patterns evolve when researchers are no longer only clicking through tools, but collaborating with systems that can suggest, reason, and act.

  * **How can interfaces help researchers understand, evaluate, and trust computational outputs?**
    * Exploring how visualization, explanation, and interaction design can bridge the gap between model outputs and scientific reasoning.

- **Examples (Projects):**

  * Redesigning existing bioinformatics tools through human-centered design principles
    *  ```#1 Galaxy workflow redesign```
    *  ```#2 UCSC Genome Browser redesign```
  * Interactive scientific visualization
     * ```#1 Biological data exploration interface```
  * Interfaces for exploring AI-generated insights
     * ```#1 AI scientific reasoning interface```

#### 3. Biological Intelligence Systems 🧬

- **How can AI systems help researchers move from biological data toward deeper understanding and discovery?**

- **Questions I explore:**

  * **How can biological foundation models be integrated into scientific workflows?**
    * Exploring how models trained on biological data can become useful components of research systems rather than standalone predictors.

  * **How can AI connect information across biological scales and data types?**
    * Exploring how AI can integrate knowledge across genomics, transcriptomics, literature, imaging, and experimental data.

  * **How can computational predictions become actionable biological insights?**
    * Exploring how researchers can move from model outputs toward hypotheses, experimental decisions, and scientific discoveries.

- **Examples (Projects):**

  * Foundation model applications in biology
     * ```#1 BioNeMo exploration```
     * ```#2 Biological embedding analysis```
  * Knowledge and literature systems
     * ```#1 Literature reasoning assistant```
  * AI-assisted biological interpretation
     * ```#1 Multiomics interpretation tool```
